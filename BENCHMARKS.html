This corrects an earlier claim on this page. Waste was said to be why speed never improved. Removing all of it moved throughput 5% — so that was wrong. Parallel wall-clock is bounded by the critical path, the slowest task in each wave, not by how many executions run. The waste was costing tokens, not time. It is still the largest efficiency gain in the series; it is just not a speed fix, and speed is bottlenecked somewhere nobody has measured yet.

Caveat: 2 parallel runs and 22 tasks, against run 4's 11 runs and 166 tasks. Zero heals is a clean binary result — none fired at all — but the per-task rates are thin.

Five measures

whole-run, all five

Run 5 was deliberately small — 2 phases of 11 tasks — and only 2 of its 5 execute runs were parallel; the other three were resumptions doing 0–1 tasks. Those inflate the task count and deflate min/task, which is why the parallel-only table above is the honest comparison.
